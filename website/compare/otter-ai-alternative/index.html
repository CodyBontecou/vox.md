On-device Otter.ai alternative for Mac and iPhone

Compare the cloud bot approach with on-device capture, see where Vox.md fits, and where Otter is the better choice.

By
Cody Bontecou
Updated
November 23, 2026
Scope
macOS 14+ or iOS 17.6+ · downloaded speech model
The short answer

If you want meeting and voice transcription that runs on your Apple device, joins no call, and saves Markdown to your own folder or Obsidian vault, Vox.md is one option. If you need a shared team workspace, a web portal, Android or Windows, stay with Otter or another cloud tool.

This comparison is published by Vox.md, which is a competing product. Statements about Vox.md follow the Vox.md docs. We ran no accuracy or speed tests.

How Otter works, in brief

Otter’s help center describes its meeting assistant, called Otter Notetaker (earlier named OtterPilot), as joining Zoom, Microsoft Teams and Google Meet meetings. After you connect a calendar it can join events that have a meeting link, or you can paste a meeting link to add it. It cannot join anonymously: it appears in the meeting as a guest participant, and the host may need to admit it. See Otter Notetaker overview and Manually add Otter Notetaker to a meeting.

Otter’s pricing page lists several plan tiers (Basic, Pro, Business and Enterprise at the time of writing). It sets out, for each plan, monthly transcription minutes, the maximum length of one conversation and the number of concurrent meetings. These limits differ by plan and can change, so check Otter’s pricing page for current limits before you decide. The same page lists Otter apps for Mac, Windows, iOS and Android, a Chrome extension and web recording.

Common reasons people look for an alternative

These are reasons a reader might have, not statistics about Otter’s users:

  • A visible bot in the call. Some people do not want an extra participant on a sensitive or informal call.
  • Plan limits. Monthly minutes and per-conversation length differ by plan, and a heavy month or a long session may not fit the plan you are on.
  • Where audio is processed. Some people prefer that recordings stay on their own computer or phone.
  • Notes as plain files. Some want a Markdown file in a folder or Obsidian vault rather than a transcript inside a vendor’s app.

If none of these apply to you, you may not need to switch.

Vox.md as an on-device option

On a Mac, switch the input mode to Meeting. Vox.md uses Apple’s ScreenCaptureKit framework to record exactly one app’s audio and your microphone as two separate tracks, then transcribes both on your Mac with Whisper (through whisper.cpp) or Parakeet (through FluidAudio). Your microphone is labelled You. Optional Identify Speakers adds anonymous, best-effort labels for several remote voices. No bot joins the call and no account is needed. macOS asks for screen and system audio recording access, which Apple explains in Control access to screen and system audio recording on Mac. Details are in the Mac meeting capture docs and the step-by-step meetings guide.

On iPhone, you can record in person or import audio and video, transcribe on the device, and optionally add speaker labels. See iPhone voice notes to Obsidian and transcribing an audio file on Mac.

Notes are standard Markdown files, saved to an Obsidian vault or a Files folder you choose through a Route. The Meeting Notes mode writes a ## Notes section and, when the text contains action items, a ## Action Items checklist. It uses Apple Intelligence where available and a deterministic formatter otherwise. For headings such as agenda and decisions, generate a meeting notes template.

Pricing: the free download includes 15 minutes of transcription and 10 successful deliveries; unlimited use is a one-time lifetime purchase with no subscription. See free use and lifetime unlock, and the App Store for localized prices.

When to stay with Otter

  • You need Windows or Android. Vox.md runs only on Apple devices; Otter lists Windows and Android apps on its pricing page.
  • You need a web portal or a team workspace. Vox.md has no shared workspace, web portal or live collaborative transcript. Its notes are files you share yourself.
  • You want an assistant to attend meetings you are not at. A notetaker that joins from your calendar is something Vox.md does not do, because it records from your own Mac.
  • Your meetings already work well in Otter and you are comfortable with how its plan handles minutes and length.

Try it on one meeting

  1. Download Vox.md on your Mac from the App Store.
  2. Open the Models screen and download a Whisper or Parakeet model. This is the one time it needs a connection. See Whisper vs Parakeet.
  3. Create a Meetings Route that writes to a folder in your vault, with the Meeting Notes mode if you want a Notes section and action items.
  4. Record a short test call after everyone on it agrees, with the input mode set to Meeting and the call app chosen.
  5. Open the Markdown note and check the labels and wording against what was said.

The full walkthrough is Transcribe Mac meetings locally into Obsidian. If you only have caption files from Zoom or Teams, convert SRT or VTT to Markdown in your browser.

Recording etiquette and consent

Recording laws and workplace policies differ by place. Tell the other participants you are recording and transcribing, and get their consent first. This is not legal advice. See Tell people you are recording.

Questions

Does it work offline after the model download?

Yes. Recording and transcription run on the device. Downloading a Whisper or Parakeet model needs a connection once, and the speaker model is also a one-time download. Saving to a cloud-synced folder syncs under that service’s terms.

Does it join Zoom?

No. Nothing joins the call. On a Mac, Vox.md records the audio of the app you choose, such as Zoom, plus your microphone.

Can I import an existing Otter export or audio file?

You can import audio or video files for local transcription. The docs do not describe importing Otter exports, so we make no claim about them. For caption files, the SRT and VTT converter runs in your browser.

Does it run on Windows?

No. Vox.md is for Apple devices only: iOS and iPadOS 17.6+, macOS 14+ and watchOS 10+.
