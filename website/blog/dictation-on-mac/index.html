How to dictate on a Mac, and a private local option

Set up Dictation in a few steps, fix it if it does not work, and decide whether you want a local model or a Markdown note instead.

By
Cody Bontecou
Updated
November 22, 2026
Requires
Vox.md: macOS 14 or later. Apple Dictation availability depends on your Mac, macOS version and language.
The short answer

Turn on Apple Dictation in System Settings > Keyboard and press the Dictation shortcut in any text field. If you want to pick a local Whisper or Parakeet model, or send dictation into Markdown notes, assign a Vox.md global shortcut.

Apple’s steps below are taken from Apple Support’s page Use Dictation on Mac, read on November 22, 2026. Menu names can differ between macOS versions, so check that page for yours. The Vox.md steps follow the global keybinds and models documentation.

How to turn on Dictation on a Mac

  1. Choose Apple menu > System Settings, then click Keyboard in the sidebar.
  2. Go to Dictation and turn it on.
  3. Shortcut: in the same place, click the Shortcut pop-up menu and choose a shortcut to start Dictation. Apple’s page doesn’t name a default, so look at what is set on your Mac.
  4. Language: click Edit next to Languages, then select a language and region. While dictating, you can switch by clicking the language next to the cursor, or by pressing the Globe key if your keyboard has one.
  5. Microphone: use the Microphone source pop-up menu to choose the microphone for Dictation. With Automatic, Apple says your Mac listens to the device you’re most likely to use.
  6. Start dictating: click in a text field, then press the Microphone key if your function row has one, use the Dictation shortcut, or choose Edit > Start Dictation.
  7. Stop: press the Escape key, the Microphone key (if available), or the Dictation shortcut.

What Apple says about processing and limits. Apple says you can check Keyboard settings to see whether general text Dictation is “processed on your device and not sent to Siri servers.” The page does not state that Dictation works offline. Apple also says you can dictate text of any length without a timeout, and that Dictation stops automatically when no speech is detected for 30 seconds. Sharing audio recordings with Apple is optional (Share Audio Recordings). Apple notes that Dictation isn’t available in all languages or regions and features may vary; see Apple’s Dictation page and the macOS feature availability page it links to. When Voice Control is on, Apple says standard macOS Dictation isn’t available.

Apple Dictation or a local dictation app

Apple Dictation is already on your Mac and is enough for many people. Reach for something else when you want to choose the speech model, keep a transcript out of Apple’s servers by design, or save speech as Markdown. Dictate into any Mac app with Vox.md

Transcribe to Clipboard is a global shortcut. You press it, speak, and press it again. Vox.md transcribes on your Mac and puts the text on the clipboard, and you paste it with Command-V. It does not type into the frontmost app by itself. It creates no note and no Library item, and it keeps no audio.

  1. Install and download a model. Install Vox.md for Mac (macOS 14 or later). On the Models screen, download a model: Whisper from Tiny (75 MB) up to Large v3 Turbo (1.6 GB), Parakeet v2 for English, or Parakeet v3 for 25 languages (about 800 MB each). You start every download, and you can delete a model later. Whisper uses Metal acceleration on supported Macs. Select the transcription language on the same screen. See Whisper vs Parakeet for how to choose.
  2. Assign the shortcut. Open Settings > Global Keybinds and assign Transcribe to Clipboard. Shortcuts are off by default. A shortcut needs a non-modifier key plus Control, Option or Command, can also include Shift, and Vox.md rejects conflicting shortcuts.
  3. Record. Click into the place you want the text, press the shortcut, and speak.
  4. Stop. Press the same shortcut again. Wait until the menu bar shows that transcribing has finished.
  5. Paste. Press Command-V.

If you need the text again, choose Copy Last Transcript from the Vox.md menu bar. The menu bar actions are listed in the menu bar documentation. The iPhone equivalent is a keyboard; see the iPhone voice-to-text keyboard guide.

Dictate into an Obsidian note or Markdown file

Choose this when the words belong in a note rather than in another app. In Settings > Global Keybinds, assign a shortcut to Selected Route or to a specific Route. The transcript then follows that Route: the daily note, the heading placement and the processing mode you set up there. This is not a paste; Vox.md delivers the text to the Route’s destination. Set up a Route in the daily note template guide and the capture templates guide. For recording calls rather than dictation, see Mac meetings into Obsidian. The free allowance includes 10 successful Capture deliveries.

What stays on the Mac

Vox.md does not send capture content to Vox.md servers. Transcription runs on your Mac. The only network use is the model download you start yourself, which uses Hugging Face or FluidAudio infrastructure. Transcription itself runs on the Mac. If a note lands in a folder that syncs through iCloud or another service, that service handles the file under its own terms. See the privacy policy and offline transcription setup. Vox.md uses whisper.cpp for Whisper and FluidAudio for Parakeet.

Mac dictation not working

Apple Dictation checks

Apple’s page If Dictation on Mac doesn’t work as expected lists these:

  • Be sure Dictation is turned on in Keyboard settings.
  • Choose the correct language and region. Dictation isn’t available in all languages or regions.
  • Be sure you’re using the correct Dictation keyboard shortcut. If another shortcut uses the same keys, change the Dictation shortcut in Keyboard > Dictation.
  • If your Mac has no built-in microphone, make sure an external microphone is connected and selected in Sound or Keyboard settings, and that its input volume is sufficient.
  • Be sure the microphone isn’t obstructed, speak clearly at a volume that isn’t too quiet or too loud, and avoid background noise.
  • You may need an internet connection; check Keyboard settings to see whether dictation is processed on your device.

Vox.md checks

  • The shortcut does nothing. Shortcuts are off by default. Assign one in Settings > Global Keybinds. Vox.md rejects conflicting shortcuts, so pick a different key combination.
  • No transcript. Make sure a model is downloaded on the Models screen and selected.
  • Wrong language. Select the language on the Models screen.
  • Nothing to paste. Wait for the menu bar to show transcribing is finished, then press Command-V, or choose Copy Last Transcript.

Questions

Does Mac dictation work offline?

Apple’s Use Dictation page doesn’t say that it does. It says you can check Keyboard settings to see whether general text Dictation is processed on your device and not sent to Siri servers. Its troubleshooting page says you may need an internet connection. After you download a model, Vox.md transcribes on your Mac.

Is there a time limit?

Apple says you can dictate text of any length without a timeout, and that Dictation stops after 30 seconds without speech. For Vox.md, the free tier includes 15 minutes of transcription, then a one-time lifetime unlock.

Can I change the shortcut?

Yes, in both. For Apple Dictation, use the Shortcut pop-up menu in System Settings > Keyboard > Dictation. For Vox.md, assign it in Settings > Global Keybinds, with a non-modifier key plus Control, Option or Command.

Does Vox.md need an account?

No.
