Brain dump app for iPhone: talk it out, get a Markdown task list

A method that works with any app, then a setup that turns what you say into - [ ] tasks in files you own, with no account.

By
Cody Bontecou
Updated
November 27, 2026
Requires
iPhone or iPad on iOS/iPadOS 17.6+ · an Obsidian vault or a Files folder · optional: Apple Intelligence hardware on iOS 26
The short answer

A brain dump means writing down everything on your mind without sorting it, and sorting it afterwards. With Vox.md on iPhone you can say it out loud. Vox.md transcribes on the device and writes the result to a Markdown note in your vault, as - [ ] tasks if you use the Todo Checklist mode. No account, and nothing is sent to Vox.md servers.

The Vox.md steps follow the documentation and the app’s shipped behavior; they were traced through documentation and source, not performed on a new device walkthrough.

What a brain dump is, and why to do it out loud

A brain dump has two separate steps. First you capture everything that is on your mind, unfiltered: tasks, worries, errands, half-ideas. Then, later, you sort it. Keeping the steps apart is the point: you do not decide what matters while you are still remembering.

Talking suits the first step. In one study of mobile text entry, speech recognition was about three times faster than typing on a phone keyboard for English (Ruan et al., “Speech Is 3x Faster than Typing for English and Mandarin Text Entry on Mobile Devices”). That is one study with one recognizer, so treat it as a reason to try, not a promise. Some people with ADHD say they use brain dumps; that is a user-reported practice, and this guide is not medical advice.

A 10-minute brain dump method (works with any app)

  1. Set a timer for 10 minutes. A fixed end makes it easy to start.
  2. Dump everything. If you get stuck, go through categories: work, home, people, money, worries, ideas.
  3. Do not edit while you dump. Repeats, fragments and half-sentences are fine.
  4. Sort afterwards. Put each item into one of four buckets: do (soon, by you), schedule (needs a date), delegate (someone else), drop (not doing it).
  5. Pick one to three for today. Leave the rest where you can find them.

Example dump (what you might say):

Need to email the landlord about the heater, buy oat milk, the quarterly report is due next week, call Sam about Saturday, I keep worrying about the car insurance renewal, maybe a newsletter idea about note-taking.

Sorted result (done by hand, after the dump):

  • Do today: email the landlord about the heater; buy oat milk
  • Schedule: the quarterly report (due next week); call Sam about Saturday
  • Delegate: none this time
  • Drop or park: the newsletter idea (move it to an ideas note); the car insurance worry becomes one task, “check renewal date”, to schedule

What to look for in a brain dump app

  • Capture speed. How many taps from the Lock Screen, the Action Button or a widget to a recording that is running?
  • Voice transcription, and where it runs. On the device, or on someone’s server?
  • Where the notes live. Inside the app, or in files you own and can open in other tools.
  • Export. Can you get everything out as plain text without a manual copy?
  • The price model. A monthly cap on dumps behaves differently from a plan without one. Read what the cap counts.

Set up a brain dump Route in Vox.md

A Route stores how a capture is processed and where it is written (see Routes in the docs).

  1. Create the Route. Open Settings → Routes and create a new route, for example “Brain dump”.
  2. Choose the processing mode. Select Todo Checklist, which produces - [ ] task items. Per the processing modes reference: with Use Apple Intelligence on (compatible iOS 26 hardware), the mode is processed on the device. With it off, voice transcripts still get the deterministic checklist formatter.
  3. Set Apply To to Voice Only, so typed Markdown is not rewritten.
  4. Choose the destination. Pick your Obsidian vault or a Files folder, then a rolling daily note or one fixed existing note such as Inbox.md (see destinations).
  5. Choose the placement. Use Heading and enter a heading such as ## Brain dump, and set a missing heading to Create Heading (see placement and headings).
  6. Optional metadata. Add tags: [braindump] as a static metadata line (see metadata).
  7. Choose a transcription backend. Automatic prefers Apple Speech on a supported iOS 26 device and language; otherwise download a Whisper or Parakeet model first (see models and offline transcription).

Start a dump in one tap

Complete microphone, backend and destination setup first, and test a short recording. What the docs list as available:

  • Control Center and Lock Screen. Vox.md capture and recording controls; Control Center controls need iOS 18. Apple explains how to add controls in Use and customize Control Center on iPhone. Depending on iOS and the app version, recording may open the app.
  • Widgets. Quick Capture and Quick Record Home Screen and Lock Screen widgets.
  • Shortcuts. Routes appear in Shortcuts, with a Record a Capture action. See Shortcuts and App Intents.
  • Apple Watch. Record on the Watch; the recording is delivered through the iPhone queue and the route you pick. See Apple Watch to Obsidian.
  • Inline voice in Quick Capture. Tap the voice button, talk, and choose Send Immediately to run the Route, or Add to Draft to review first. See inline voice controls.

Sort the dump in Obsidian

With the Route above, the example dump can land in your daily note like this (an illustration: how the text splits into items depends on the transcript):

## Brain dump
- [ ] Email the landlord about the heater
- [ ] Buy oat milk
- [ ] The quarterly report is due next week
- [ ] Call Sam about Saturday
- [ ] Car insurance renewal
- [ ] Newsletter idea about note-taking

Then sort by hand: check off what is done (- [x]), add due dates (the Markdown checklist maker can add them in Tasks, Dataview or Reminder format), and move items into project notes. Obsidian documents the syntax under Task lists and the daily note behavior in Daily notes. To list open tasks across notes, third-party community plugins such as Tasks or Dataview can query them; they are separate from Obsidian and Vox.md. For a rolling-note setup, see append to an Obsidian daily note and the daily note template; for an inbox section, see Obsidian capture templates.

Limits

  • Vox.md does not schedule tasks, estimate time, send reminders, or sort items into categories for you. The sorting stays yours.
  • The checklist split depends on the transcript and on whether Apple Intelligence is available. Review it.
  • Recording, transcription and Capture need Vox.md Unlimited: a weekly or yearly subscription with a 7-day free trial for eligible accounts, or a one-time lifetime purchase. Prices: see the App Store. See the docs.
  • Transcription runs on your device, and the audio is not sent to Vox.md servers. Downloading a Whisper or Parakeet model contacts Hugging Face. If your vault syncs, its provider syncs the file under its own terms.

FAQ

Is a brain dump the same as journaling?

No. A journal is reflective writing; a brain dump is unsorted capture followed by sorting. See keeping a private voice journal for the journal setup.

Can I do it offline?

Yes, after a transcription model is downloaded. See offline transcription on iPhone.

Does it work on Mac?

Yes. The Mac app can assign global keyboard shortcuts to a Route (Settings → Global Keybinds), then transcribes to your Route’s destination. See dictation on a Mac.

Can I type instead of talk?

Yes, in Quick Capture. Todo Checklist is applied to typed text only if Apply To is Voice & Text and Use Apple Intelligence is on. For general dictation, see speech to text on iPhone.
