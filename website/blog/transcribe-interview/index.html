How to transcribe an interview privately, with speaker labels

Consent, recording, transcription and a review pass, plus an on-device route so participant audio is not uploaded to a transcription service.

By
Cody Bontecou
Updated
November 24, 2026
Requires
Vox.md: iOS or iPadOS 17.6+ or macOS 14+ · downloaded speech model for Whisper or Parakeet · writable destination folder
The short answer

Five steps: 1) get consent and say how the audio will be processed and stored; 2) record clearly with a test first; 3) transcribe; 4) review the text against the audio; 5) label the speakers and save. You can do the whole thing on your own device with Vox.md on iPhone, iPad or Mac, so the recording is not uploaded to a transcription service. Speaker labels are optional, anonymous and best effort (“Speaker 1”, “Speaker 2”); you rename them by hand.

The method works with any tool. The Vox.md steps follow the models and Mac documentation and the app’s shipped behavior. Outside sources are linked where they are used, and the example note is illustrative, not a real interview.

Before you record

Consent and data handling. Tell the participant how the audio will be processed and stored, and get their agreement before you record. The Centre College Oral History Guide says interviewers should make sure narrators voluntarily consent, explain the purpose of the interview, and make clear their options, including restricting access or choosing anonymity. Check your ethics board or data policy too: many ask where participant audio is processed and stored, so decide your answer before the first interview.

Recording setup. Harvard Library’s Recording & Transcription guide advises spending time on good audio capture first because it makes transcription easier. In practice:

  • Use one microphone per speaker if you can, and sit close to each.
  • Pick a quiet room and switch off anything that rings or hums.
  • Record a short test and play it back before the real interview.

Verbatim or clean?

Decide the style before you transcribe, and keep it the same across a project. The Centre College guide’s Transcribing page describes verbatim as word for word, including filler sounds, pauses, interruptions and laughter, and says historians prefer it because it captures the most nuance. “Clean verbatim” and “edited” are common names for lighter styles; the table shows how they usually differ. Which one you need is set by your project, publisher or ethics board.

Common transcript styles
StyleWhat it keepsTypical fit
VerbatimEverything said: fillers, false starts, pauses, non-speech soundsOral history and research where wording and manner of speaking are data
Clean verbatimThe words said, with fillers and false starts removedMost interview analysis and quotation where meaning matters more than delivery
EditedReworded for reading: grammar tidied, passages cutPublication drafts, only with the speaker’s approval where your project requires it

Automatic transcription does not make a style choice for you. You still apply it in the review pass.

In person on iPhone or iPad

  1. Choose a transcription model. Automatic prefers Apple Speech on a supported iOS 26 device and language. You can instead download Whisper (Tiny to Large v3 Turbo), Parakeet v2 (English) or Parakeet v3 (25 languages). Downloading a model is your choice and the only network step. Whisper vs Parakeet helps you choose, and offline transcription on iPhone covers preparing it.
  2. Make an “Interviews” Route. In Settings → Routes, create a Route named Interviews. Choose a destination folder (an Obsidian vault or a Files folder) and New Note, so each capture becomes its own file. Optionally add static frontmatter, one key: value per line, such as project: housing-study. The properties generator builds valid frontmatter. Keep participant codes out of static metadata, since it applies to every note from the Route.
  3. Turn on Identify Speakers. It is an opt-in setting on each Route. The first time it is used it prepares a separate speaker model, so do that ahead of the interview, not in front of the participant. After that it runs locally. The open-source FluidAudio library is used for local speaker processing.
  4. Do a 30-second test with two voices. Record a short exchange with someone, and check that the note arrives in the destination folder and shows two labels.
  5. Record, or import. Record from the Vox.md composer, or use the import control for an existing audio or video file (how to import a video). Choose Add to Draft if you want to read the text before it is delivered, or Send Immediately to deliver when transcription finishes.
  6. Review in Library. Library shows each capture and, if speaker identification could not run, the reason.

With Identify Speakers on, each turn in the saved text gets its own label line, followed by that turn’s text on the next line, and turns are separated by a blank line:

Speaker 1:
Thanks for agreeing to talk with me today.

Speaker 2:
Happy to. Where would you like me to start?

Labels are anonymous and best effort. Vox.md does not recognise people by name. It is also available for Apple Watch transcript delivery and in the Mac app.

On a Mac

  • Recording on a Mac. For the built-in recorders and for capturing one app’s sound, see how to record audio on a Mac.
  • An existing file. Use Import Audio… in Vox.md for Mac, which accepts audio and movie files. The steps are in transcribe an audio file on Mac. Turn on Identify Speakers on the Route for anonymous labels.
  • A remote interview. Meeting capture records one app’s audio and your microphone as separate stems. You appear as “You” and the other side is labelled as a remote speaker; speaker identification can split several remote voices further. Setup is in transcribe Mac meetings into Obsidian. Tell remote participants you are recording, as in the consent step above.
  • A caption file. If Zoom or Teams already gave you an .srt or .vtt file, the SRT and VTT converter turns it into text or Markdown in your browser.

The Meeting Notes processing mode exists but restructures text into notes. For verbatim research transcripts, leave the Route on Keep Original.

The review pass

Automatic transcripts need checking. Harvard’s guide says, as with any automatically generated transcript, you need to revise it manually, and the Centre College guide says automated transcripts are not a replacement for a human’s work.

  1. Listen again at normal or slower speed while reading, and fix names, terms, numbers and dates.
  2. Apply your style from the verbatim or clean choice above.
  3. Rename the speakers. Use find and replace to turn Speaker 1 into your interviewer code and Speaker 2 into the participant code, for example P03.
  4. Anonymise names, places and other identifying details as your consent and data plan require.
  5. Keep the original audio according to your data plan.

A finished note might look like this (invented sample text):

---
participant: P03
date: 2026-11-24
consent: recorded
---

Interviewer:
Thanks for agreeing to talk with me today.

P03:
Happy to. Where would you like me to start?

Troubleshooting

  • No speaker labels. The plain transcript is always kept, and Library shows why identification was skipped. Reasons include: no timing information in the transcript; timing that doesn’t cover the whole transcript; no distinct speakers detected; model storage unavailable; the model couldn’t be prepared; or processing failed. Check that Identify Speakers is on for the Route and that the speaker model was prepared.
  • Speakers merged or split wrongly. Record closer to each voice, use separate microphones if you can, and correct the labels by hand.
  • A long recording. Larger models take longer. Try a smaller model first, and keep the device charged.
  • A language Parakeet v2 doesn’t cover. Parakeet v2 is English only. Use Parakeet v3 (25 languages) or Whisper; see Models.
  • No active plan. Recording, transcription and Capture need Vox.md Unlimited. The App Store price is authoritative.

FAQ

Can I transcribe an interview offline?

Yes, once a model is prepared. Model downloads and speaker-model preparation need a connection once; transcription then runs on the device. See offline transcription on iPhone.

Does Vox.md upload my recording?

No. Transcription and speaker labelling run on your device, and the file is written only to the folder you pick. If that folder syncs (iCloud Drive, for example), the sync provider holds the file under its own terms. See the privacy policy.

Can it tell who is speaking by name?

No. Labels are anonymous (“Speaker 1”, “Speaker 2”) and best effort. You rename them yourself.

How long does it take to transcribe an interview by hand?

The National Park Service’s Interview Transcribing page says on average 6–8 hours per hour of recorded interview.
