How to record audio on a Mac: your voice or an app’s sound

Apple’s built-in recorders first, with the Apple Support page for each, then a local route for app audio, a kept audio file and an optional on-device transcript.

By
Cody Bontecou
Updated
November 26, 2026
Requires
macOS 14+ · microphone access · a writable folder
The short answer

To record your voice, use QuickTime Player (File → New Audio Recording) or Voice Memos, which are both built in. To record what an app plays, or the app and your microphone together, you need a tool that captures app audio. Vox.md for Mac does this on your Mac and can save the audio with a Markdown note and a transcript.

The menu names below come from Apple Support’s pages, linked in each section. Your menus can differ a little by macOS version. The Vox.md steps follow the Mac workspace documentation and the meeting capture documentation.

Record audio with QuickTime Player

These steps follow Apple’s Record audio in QuickTime Player on Mac.

  1. Open QuickTime Player and choose File → New Audio Recording.
  2. Click the arrow next to the Record button to open the options menu. Under Microphone, choose the input if you have more than one. Under Quality, choose a setting; Apple notes that maximum quality produces uncompressed files that can use a lot of storage.
  3. Click the Record button to start and the Stop button when you are done. Apple says you can pause by Option-clicking the button and click it again to resume.
  4. Choose File → Save to name the recording and choose where it goes.

QuickTime Player does not write a transcript, and Apple’s page describes choosing a microphone rather than capturing an app’s sound.

Record with Voice Memos on Mac

Apple’s Create a recording on Mac gives these steps.

  1. Open the Voice Memos app.
  2. Click the Record button. You can record with the built-in microphone, a supported headset or an external microphone.
  3. Click Pause to pause and Resume to continue.
  4. Click Done to save the recording.

With iCloud turned on for Voice Memos and the same Apple Account on each device, recordings appear on your Mac, iPhone and iPad; see Apple’s See your recordings on all your Apple devices. To turn memos into text, see transcribe Voice Memos on iPhone. Apple also describes a transcription view in View a Voice Memos transcription on Mac.

Record screen and audio with Shift-Command-5

Apple’s Take screenshots or screen recordings on Mac says to press Shift-Command-5 to open Screenshot and show its tools. Choose a screen recording tool, then open the Options pop-up menu. Under Microphone, select the microphone you want so a checkmark appears next to it; select None to turn it off. The options menu also covers things like where the recording is saved.

Apple’s page describes the microphone as the audio option. It does not describe recording an app’s own sound, so this guide does not claim the toolbar does.

Record the sound an app plays

The built-in recorders above are described by Apple in terms of a microphone. If you need the sound a particular app plays, such as a lecture stream, a call or a video, you need software that can capture app audio. The route below does not use a virtual audio driver.

macOS gates this capability with a privacy permission. Apple explains it in Control access to screen and system audio recording on Mac: some apps can record the screen and audio on your Mac, and you control which ones in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Screen & System Audio Recording.

How Vox.md does it. Meeting mode uses Apple’s ScreenCaptureKit in single-application mode:

  1. Switch the input mode from Microphone to Meeting.
  2. Start recording. macOS shows its content picker; pick exactly one application.
  3. Your microphone is recorded as a separate track, and Vox.md never records its own audio.
  4. Press Stop. Each track is transcribed on your Mac, and a playback mix of both is kept as the recording’s audio.

This is one chosen app plus your microphone, not all of the sound your Mac makes. Meeting capture cannot be paused. For the full setup, including Routes, speaker labels and recovery, see transcribe Mac meetings locally into Obsidian.

Ask before you record. Recording laws and workplace rules differ by place. Tell other people when you are recording them and get their consent. This is not legal advice.

Record your voice into a note with Vox.md

For your own voice, Vox.md for Mac records from the microphone into a note rather than a loose file.

  1. In the Capture workspace, choose Add to Draft before you record, so the transcript goes into the composer for review instead of being sent right away.
  2. Record. Use Pause and Resume in the status bar to record one note in pieces. Paused audio is excluded from the recording.
  3. In the Route, set Save Audio to Alongside Note or Attachments Folder so the audio file is kept. See audio retention.
  4. Embed the kept audio in the note with Obsidian syntax such as ![[recording.m4a]].

If you download a Whisper or Parakeet model, Vox.md also gives you a transcript on your device. The model reference lists sizes and languages, and Whisper vs Parakeet helps you choose. If you only want to speak text into other apps, see dictation on Mac. Setup details are in the Mac workspace documentation, and the free tier is described in free use and lifetime unlock.

No sound or a silent file

  • Input device. Choose Apple menu → System Settings → Sound → Input and select the device you want. See Apple’s Change the sound input settings on Mac.
  • Microphone permission. In System Settings → Privacy & Security → Microphone, turn the app on. See Apple’s Control access to the microphone on Mac.
  • App sound. In Privacy & Security → Screen & System Audio Recording, turn the recording app on; see Apple’s page. In Vox.md Meeting mode, check that both level meters move while the app plays.
