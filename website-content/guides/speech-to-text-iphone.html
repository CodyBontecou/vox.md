How to turn on speech to text on iPhone

  1. Open the Settings app, tap General, then tap Keyboard.
  2. Turn on Enable Dictation. If a prompt appears, tap Enable Dictation again.
  3. In a text field, tap to place the insertion point where you want the text.
  4. Tap the microphone (Dictate) key on the onscreen keyboard, or in any text field where it appears. When the microphone indicator appears near the insertion point, start speaking.
  5. Stop: tap the microphone key again, say “Stop dictation,” or stay silent for 30 seconds, after which Dictation stops automatically.

To turn Dictation off, go back to Settings > General > Keyboard and turn off Enable Dictation. Apple notes that the keyboard stays open during Dictation, so you can switch between touch and voice. If you use a keyboard for another language, Apple says you can use Dictation in that language too. Apple also says cellular data charges may apply when using Dictation.

Punctuation, emoji and new lines

  • Punctuation: say the name of the mark, such as “exclamation point.”
  • Emoji: say the name of an emoji, such as “heart emoji” or “smiley face emoji.”
  • Line break: say “new line” or “new paragraph.”
  • Spelling: say something like “Let’s invite Steven S-T-E-P-H-E-N.”
  • Edit: say “select” or “delete” followed by the phrase you want to change, and “undo” or “redo” to reverse or repeat a command.

In supported languages, Apple says Dictation automatically inserts commas, periods and question marks as you dictate. To turn that off, go to Settings > General > Keyboard and turn off Auto-Punctuation. Apple lists the full set in Commands for dictating text on iPhone.

Does iPhone dictation work offline?

Apple says: “Dictation is processed on your iPhone in many languages, and no internet connection is required. When you dictate in a search field, your dictated text may be sent to the search engine to process the search.” Apple also says Dictation may not be available in all languages or in all countries or regions, and features may vary. Check Apple’s iOS and iPadOS Feature Availability page for your language.

Apple’s page adds that with Apple Intelligence in iOS 27, dictation uses an on-device model to improve spelling, punctuation and capitalization. It states that this is available in English on iPhone 17 Pro, iPhone 17 Pro Max, iPhone Air, or later, and that Apple Intelligence on iOS 27 or later is available on an Apple Intelligence-enabled iPhone 15 Pro, iPhone 15 Pro Max, or iPhone 16 model or later. Apple Intelligence is not available in all languages or regions, and usage limits may apply.

Use a local model in any app with the Vox.md keyboard

  1. Prepare transcription. In Vox.md, keep Automatic on a supported device, or download and select Whisper or Parakeet in Settings > Models.
  2. Add the keyboard. In iOS Settings, go to General > Keyboard > Keyboards > Add New Keyboard and choose Vox.md.
  3. Turn on Allow Full Access. Vox.md uses it for its on-device app and keyboard workflow, shared state and optional haptics, and does not upload keystrokes, dictated text, transcripts or microphone audio to Vox.md servers. See the keyboard setup docs.
  4. Switch and dictate. In a text field, use the globe key to select Vox.md, then tap the microphone.

For the full walkthrough, see the iPhone voice-to-text keyboard guide. To pick a model, read Whisper vs Parakeet.

What stays on the iPhone

Vox.md does not send capture content to Vox.md servers. Transcription runs on your iPhone. The only network use is the model download you start yourself, which uses Hugging Face or FluidAudio infrastructure. If a note lands in a folder that syncs through iCloud or another service, that service handles the file under its own terms. See the privacy policy. Vox.md uses whisper.cpp for Whisper and FluidAudio for Parakeet.

iPhone dictation not working

Apple Dictation checks

These come from Dictate text on iPhone:

  • If you don’t see the microphone key, go to Settings > General > Keyboard and turn on Enable Dictation. Tap Enable Dictation if a prompt appears.
  • Dictation may not be available in all languages or regions. See Apple’s feature availability page.
  • Dictation stops automatically after 30 seconds of silence. Tap the microphone key to start again.
  • In a search field, dictated text may be sent to the search engine.

Vox.md checks

  • The keyboard says Enable Full Access. Open iOS Settings > General > Keyboard > Keyboards > Vox.md and turn on Allow Full Access, then return to the app once.
  • The keyboard says Open Vox.md. If listening is not active, the keyboard opens Vox.md once to start listening.
  • No transcript. Make sure a model is fully downloaded and selected in Settings > Models.
  • Automatic is unavailable. It needs a supported iOS 26 device and language; choose a Whisper or Parakeet model instead.

More in the Vox.md troubleshooting docs. On an iPad with a keyboard attached, see dictating with a Magic Keyboard.

Questions

Is iPhone dictation free?

Apple’s page doesn’t mention a charge, though it says cellular data charges may apply. Vox.md includes 15 minutes of transcription, then a one-time lifetime unlock with no subscription.

Does it work offline?

Apple says Dictation is processed on the iPhone in many languages with no internet connection required, except that text dictated into a search field may be sent to the search engine. Once a model is downloaded, Vox.md transcribes on the iPhone.

Is there a time limit?

Apple says Dictation stops automatically after 30 seconds of silence. Its page doesn’t state a maximum length.

Why does the Vox.md keyboard need Full Access?

The current keyboard workflow needs it to use shared App Group state and coordinate recording with the main app. Vox.md does not upload keystrokes, dictated text, transcripts or microphone audio to Vox.md servers.

Can I dictate into Obsidian?

Yes. Use a Vox.md Route to deliver the transcript to a note in your vault, as in the voice notes guide.
