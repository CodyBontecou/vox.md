The short answer

Does Wispr Flow work offline?

Wispr’s own pages describe a cloud service. Short quotes, each checked on November 28, 2026:

  • Internet connection. Wispr’s What is Flow page says “transcription requires an internet connection.” The same page lists Mac, Windows, iPhone and Android as supported platforms.
  • Cloud transcription. Wispr’s Data Controls page says “Transcription always occurs on the cloud.” It also says Wispr maintains “zero data retention agreements with all third-party AI providers.”
  • Turning off data sharing does not make it local. Wispr’s privacy settings article says that turning off “Improve the model for everyone” does not stop cloud processing: “Turning it off does not stop cloud processing.” It describes a separate Dictation cloud storage switch for keeping transcripts, audio and history on Wispr’s servers.
  • Third-party LLMs and US processing. Wispr’s privacy policy says “we may share your data with third-party LLMs in order to provide certain features,” and that Wispr transfers data to the United States and processes it there.

Wispr also publishes a help article on Turtle Mode on iOS. When we checked it on November 28, 2026, the page at that address covered retrying a failed transcription, and said retry needs an internet connection. It no longer described on-device drafts, so this page makes no claim about them. If you need to know whether any Wispr feature works with no connection, ask Wispr or read its current help center.

Set up on-device dictation on iPhone and iPad

The Vox.md keyboard dictates into any text field that accepts a standard iOS keyboard, per the keyboard documentation.

  1. Pick a backend. In Vox.md, keep Automatic selected on a supported iOS 26 device, which uses Apple Speech. Otherwise open Settings > Models and download Whisper or Parakeet. See Whisper vs Parakeet to choose. Automatic never downloads a model silently.
  2. Download a model for offline use. Downloads need a connection once and run only when you ask for them. After the selected backend is ready, transcription runs on the device. You can delete a model later.
  3. Add the keyboard. In iOS Settings, go to General > Keyboard > Keyboards > Add New Keyboard and choose Vox.md.
  4. Turn on Allow Full Access. It is required: the current keyboard workflow needs it to use shared App Group state and coordinate recording with the main app. iOS labels the permission broadly. Vox.md does not upload keystrokes, dictated text, transcripts or microphone audio to Vox.md servers.
  5. Dictate. In a text field, use the globe key to select Vox.md, then tap the microphone. With Automatic, finalized text is inserted while you speak. With Whisper or Parakeet, the transcript is inserted after you stop.

Keyboard dictation needs Vox.md Unlimited. For step-by-step help, see speech to text on iPhone and on iPad; they also cover Apple Dictation, the free built-in option. To check the offline workflow end to end, see offline transcription on iPhone.

Set up on Mac

On Mac, Vox.md does not type into the front app. Transcribe to Clipboard is a global shortcut: press it, speak, press it again, and the text lands on the clipboard for you to paste with Command-V.

  1. Install Vox.md for Mac (macOS 14 or later). On the Models screen, download and select a Whisper or Parakeet model, and choose the transcription language.
  2. Open Settings > Global Keybinds and assign Transcribe to Clipboard. Shortcuts are off by default. A shortcut needs a non-modifier key plus Control, Option or Command, may also include Shift, and Vox.md rejects conflicts.
  3. Click where you want the text, press the shortcut, and speak. Press the same shortcut to stop and wait for the menu bar to finish transcribing.
  4. Press Command-V. If you need the text again, choose Copy Last Transcript from the menu bar.

Transcribe to Clipboard creates no note, Library item or retained audio. The full walkthrough, with Apple’s own Dictation steps, is in how to dictate on a Mac. The Mac documentation describes global keybinds in detail.

Dictate into notes

This is what Vox.md adds beyond dictation: the words can land in a Markdown file you own. In Settings > Global Keybinds, assign a shortcut to Selected Route or to a specific Route instead. A Route can append to a rolling daily note, or write a new note, in an Obsidian vault or another writable folder. It can also place the entry at the top, bottom, or beneath a heading. On iPhone and iPad, voice captured in the app follows the same Routes.

Route processing modes are Keep Original, Clean Prose, Todo Checklist, Meeting Notes and Custom Instruction. Clean Prose and Custom Instruction use Apple Intelligence; without it they keep the original text. For the setup, see append to an Obsidian daily note, or compare approaches in Vox.md vs Obsidian Quick Capture.

Other local options

Apple Dictation is built into iPhone, iPad and Mac, and costs nothing extra. Whether it processes speech on the device depends on your device and language; Apple’s pages for iPhone and Mac say what to check in Keyboard settings. Other apps may also transcribe locally; check each app’s own site for what it states.

When to keep Wispr Flow

  • You use Windows. Wispr lists Windows as a platform. Vox.md has no Windows app.
  • You need text typed straight into any Mac app. Wispr says it inserts text into the selected text field. Vox.md on Mac copies to the clipboard, and you paste.
  • You want heavy cloud AI rewriting. Wispr’s pages describe cloud processing and third-party LLMs. Vox.md’s on-device processing is narrower, and its Apple Intelligence modes need compatible hardware.
  • You do not mind cloud transcription. If your audio can go to a cloud service under Wispr’s terms, the offline difference may not matter to you.
