The short answer

Vox.md for Mac can record one app’s audio and your microphone as separate tracks, transcribe both on your Mac, and save a speaker-labelled Markdown note to an Obsidian vault or any folder. There’s no meeting bot and no account.

This guide walks through one working setup, from a Route that writes to Meetings/ in your vault to a finished note. The steps follow the Mac meeting capture documentation and the app’s shipped behavior. The Markdown shown below is an illustrative example, not a recording of a real call.

1. What you need

  • A Mac running macOS 14 or later with the Vox.md Mac app, installed from the same App Store listing as the iPhone app.
  • Microphone access for Vox.md, so your own voice is recorded.
  • Screen and system audio recording access. Meeting capture uses Apple’s ScreenCaptureKit framework to record the audio of the app you choose. If macOS asks, allow it. You can review both permissions in System Settings → Privacy & Security; Apple explains the setting in Control access to screen and system audio recording on Mac.
  • A downloaded Whisper or Parakeet model. Open the Mac Models screen, download a model, and set the transcription language. The model reference lists sizes and languages, and Whisper vs Parakeet helps you choose. Whisper runs through whisper.cpp and Parakeet through FluidAudio.
  • A writable folder, such as your Obsidian vault or a Meetings folder inside it.
  • Just need a plain recording? See how to record audio on a Mac.

Optional Apple Intelligence processing, which the Meeting Notes mode uses to restructure the transcript, needs a compatible Mac running macOS 26. Without it, Vox.md uses a deterministic formatter instead, so the Route still works.

2. Prepare a meeting Route

A Route decides how a capture is processed and where its Markdown is written. Some Mac versions label Routes as Capture Presets in Settings.

  1. Open Settings in the Mac app and create a new Route named something like Meetings.
  2. In its destination, choose your vault as the folder. For the note target, choose New Note so every meeting gets its own file.
  3. Enter a path template such as Meetings/{date}-{hour}{minute}.md. Including the time keeps two meetings on the same day in separate notes. See Obsidian capture templates for more path and token recipes.
  4. Choose a processing Mode. Keep Original delivers the labelled transcript as recognized. Meeting Notes produces a ## Notes section and, when the text contains action items, a ## Action Items checklist. Want headings for an agenda and decisions too? Generate a meeting notes template.
  5. Optional: turn on Identify Speakers if several remote people talk. The speaker model downloads the first time this Route uses it.
  6. Optional: set Save Audio to keep the recording alongside the note or in an attachments folder. Leave it off if you only want the text.

3. Record the call

  1. Join the call in its own app: Zoom, Microsoft Teams, FaceTime, or a browser running Google Meet.
  2. In the Vox.md Capture window, select the Meetings Route.
  3. Switch the audio source to Meeting; the default is Microphone. Depending on your app version, that control is in the Capture window or under Capture → Audio Source in the menu bar.
  4. Start recording. macOS shows its content picker; pick exactly one application. Vox.md never records its own audio. For a call in a browser, pick the browser. Its audio is recorded as a whole, so mute other tabs that play sound.
  5. While you record, the status bar shows live System and Mic level meters, the status of each source, and any capture warnings. Check that both meters move when people speak.

Meeting capture cannot be paused. Microphone recordings have Pause and Resume, but a meeting runs from start to stop. You also cannot switch to a different app partway through; stop and start a new recording instead.

4. Stop, transcribe and deliver

Press Stop when the call ends. Vox.md transcribes the two tracks separately on your Mac with the model you chose, then interleaves them on a shared timeline. Your microphone track is labelled You, and the application track is labelled as a remote speaker, such as Speaker 2. With Keep Original, a short meeting might produce a note body like this illustrative example:

You:
Thanks for joining. Can we start with the launch checklist?

Speaker 2:
Sure. The release notes are ready, but we still need screenshots.

You:
I’ll take the screenshots by Friday.

With Identify Speakers on, Vox.md tries to split the remote track into more labels, such as Speaker 2 and Speaker 3. These labels are anonymous and best-effort. If identification cannot run, you keep the normal transcript and the app shows why.

Separate tracks are the reason the You label works. Your voice comes from the microphone track and the other participants come from the app’s track, so Vox.md does not have to guess which side of the call said what from a single mixed recording. Labels can still be wrong. For example, the microphone can pick up audio from your speakers if you are not wearing headphones.

What happens next depends on the recording option you chose before you started:

  • Add to Draft puts the transcript in the Capture composer. Review and edit it, then press Command-Return to send it through the Route.
  • Send Immediately delivers the note through the Route as soon as transcription finishes.

The transcript also follows your clipboard settings, like any other recording. A playback mix of both tracks is kept as the recording’s audio, and it is saved with the note only if the Route’s Save Audio setting keeps audio. Open the note in Obsidian once to confirm the path and labels look the way you expect.

5. If the recording is interrupted

Both tracks are written to disk in durable chunks with a recovery manifest. If the stream stops, the microphone is unplugged, or Vox.md is force quit, the chunks recorded so far can still be finalized and transcribed. Recovery runs when the recording queue resumes, for example the next time you open Vox.md.

A note from an incomplete capture starts with a warning line that says which part was missing, so you know the transcript may have gaps.

6. Tell people you are recording

Recording laws and workplace policies differ by place and organization. Some require everyone’s consent. Tell the other participants that you are recording and transcribing, and get their consent before you start. This is not legal advice; check the rules that apply to you.

8. Limits to know before a long call

  • One app per recording. Meeting capture records a single application plus your microphone.
  • Labels are best-effort. Speaker labels depend on clean, separate audio, and speaker identification can merge or split voices.
  • Quality depends on the model and language. Larger models use more storage and time. Review important wording against the audio before you rely on it.
  • Recording needs Vox.md Unlimited. Eligible accounts get a 7-day free trial; see plans and pricing.
  • Some things leave your Mac. Recording and transcription stay on the device, and audio is not uploaded for transcription. Downloading a Whisper or Parakeet model contacts Hugging Face, as described in the privacy policy, and the speaker model is also a one-time download. If your vault syncs with iCloud Drive or Obsidian Sync, the finished note syncs under that service’s terms. Vox.md writes files; sync is your choice.
